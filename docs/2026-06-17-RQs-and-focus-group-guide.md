# Research questions and focus-group guide (theory-grounded)

Grounded in Colquitt (2001, four justice dimensions) and Tyler & Blader (2003, legitimacy of
authority). Participant-facing questions are plain and non-leading; bracketed tags are for the
researcher only. Non-leading rule: the moderator never says agency, community, fairness, trust,
legitimacy, or sustainability before a participant does.

## Research questions

**Overarching.** How do AI-assisted decision prototypes in a sustainability context (household
cooling under a shared limit) shape people's experience of AI-made decisions and their willingness
to accept an AI as a decision-making authority?

- **RQ1 - Design check.** Do participants perceive the four prototypes as differing in how much say
  they have (agency) and whether others are involved (community)?
- **RQ2 - Descriptive.** How do the prototypes shape perceived control, fairness (procedural,
  distributive, interpersonal, informational), trust, sense of community, and acceptance?
- **RQ3 - AI as authority (headline).** Under what conditions do people grant an AI the legitimacy
  to decide for them, and which fairness dimensions carry that legitimacy when the decider is an AI
  rather than a person?
- **RQ4 - Sustainability and the collective.** How do collective and sustainability framings affect
  acceptance and reflection on one's own energy use?
- **RQ5 - Method (pilot).** How does the four-prototype design-fiction method perform as a research
  instrument?

## Expectations (sensitizing, not for inferential testing)

- **H1 baseline.** The outcome feels unfavourable across all four (you never get your preferred cool).
- **H2 agency -> acceptance.** More voice/control raises acceptance and perceived fairness for the
  same outcome (Tyler: procedure over outcome).
- **H3 community buffers, and prompts collective reflection.** Shared framing supplies group-based
  legitimacy that buffers low agency (C accepted more than D) and prompts more reflection on
  collective and sustainable impact than private framing.
- **H4 AI-authority transfer (novel).** Relational fairness signals (voice-as-respect, interpersonal
  treatment) may confer less legitimacy from an AI than from a person.
- **H6 method.** Four probes yield richer, more distinct reflection than one.

## Block 1 - Mapping and first impressions (RQ1, RQ5)

Five minutes individual placement on the printed two-axis map, then group discussion.

1. Walk me through your map. Why did you place each version where you did? [RQ1]
2. Which were hardest to place? Which easiest? What made the difference? [RQ1, RQ5]
3. What were the biggest differences you noticed across the four? Point to specific parts of the
   interfaces. [RQ1]
4. Tell me about a moment in any version where the way it behaved stood out, felt right, or felt
   wrong. What happened, and what did you think? [RQ2 - surfaces procedural/interpersonal reactions
   without naming them]

## Block 2 - Control, trust, and going along with it (RQ2, RQ3)

5. How much say did you feel you had across the four? How did that feel? Point to the differences.
   [RQ2 - procedural voice/control]
6. How did it feel when a version made the decision for you? [RQ2, RQ3 - acceptance of AI authority]
7. In the versions where you could give input, did it feel like the system actually took you into
   account, or just logged it? [RQ3 - Tyler's voice-as-respect, tested on a machine, the transfer test]
8. If one of these were really running in 2033, which would you go along with, and which not? What
   gave you that feeling? [RQ3 - legitimacy and trust]
9. If a person had made the same call instead, a building manager, or a group of neighbours, would
   you accept it more or less? Why? [RQ3 - AI vs human authority, the swap]

## Block 3 - Others, and what is fair (RQ2, RQ4)

10. In the versions that showed what your neighbours had, gave up, or decided, versus the ones where
    it was just you, did you feel differently? Did your own choices change? [RQ4 - community, identity]
11. Imagine the building is short of power on the worst day and cooling has to be cut. Who should
    decide, and how? Talk it through together. [RQ4 - collective legitimacy, deliberation]
12. As a group, order the four from most to least fair. You have to agree on the order. [RQ2 -
    forces justification of distributive and procedural fairness in participants' own words]

## Block 4 - Sustainability, and where AI authority ends (RQ3, RQ4)

13. Did anything in any version make you think about your own energy use, or how you cool your home?
    Which one, and what specifically? [RQ4 - sustainability reflection]
14. When would you accept an AI making this kind of decision for you? Under what conditions? [RQ3 -
    the headline, conditions for legitimacy]
15. Does it change if the decision is for a shared goal, or to save energy? [RQ4 - collective and
    sustainability legitimacy]
16. Are there decisions in any of these versions an AI should not be making? Which, and why? [RQ3 -
    the limits of AI authority]
17. Take the version you would least accept. Change one thing so you could live with it. [RQ3 -
    generative; reveals which fairness dimension they reach for, voice, explanation, appeal, a human
    in the loop]

## Block 5 - Method (pilot only) (RQ5)

18. How did seeing four versions change your thinking compared with one?
19. What should change before we run this with more people? Should the versions be more distinct?
20. Did it feel exhausting?
21. How did it feel to know these were mostly AI-created?

## Coverage check (which construct each block reaches)

- Procedural justice (voice, control, appeal): Q5, Q7, Q17.
- Distributive justice (fair outcome/share): Q12, and Q10 in the shared cells.
- Interpersonal + informational justice (treatment, explanation): Q4, Q7 (agent reactions / trust
  in ARKI).
- Legitimacy and acceptance of authority (Tyler): Q6, Q8, Q9, Q14, Q16.
- Identity and community (group engagement): Q10, Q11.
- Sustainability behaviour and collective goal: Q13, Q15.
- Design check and method: Q1-Q3, Q18-Q21.
