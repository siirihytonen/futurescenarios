# Smart-grid benchmark: real systems -> the four futures

Why benchmark: design fiction works by extrapolating a real trend ONE step past the present, so the
world feels inevitable but not mundane. If the prototypes only show what already exists, they stop
being futures. This file pins what is real now (China and Europe) and the speculative jump each
cell should take past it.

## What is real now

China (top-down, automatic):
- The NDRC plans for at least five large language models embedded in the power grid and generation
  by 2027. AI in Shanghai, Jiangsu and Guangdong predicts demand, dispatches power and steers
  consumers off peak, expected to cut peak demand ~3.5 GW in 2026. Shenzhen used AI 150+ times in
  2023-2025 to cut load at peak. The direction of travel is automatic load control, the grid acting
  on homes rather than asking them. (dialogue.earth, eco-business.com)

Europe (opt-in, rights-based):
- Smart meters are mandated; consumers get near-real-time data and can take dynamic-price
  contracts and provide flexibility (demand response) when the operator asks. It is consent-based
  and incentive-based, not compulsory. Granular meter data is protected under GDPR and the
  Cybersecurity Act; the EU Data Act governs flexibility participation. Energy communities allow
  collective self-consumption and local sharing. (energy.ec.europa.eu, EU Data Act article 2025)

The contrast in one line: China is moving toward the grid deciding and acting; Europe is built on
the household consenting and choosing. That gap is your design space.

## The governance spectrum your four futures already sit on

Your A->B->C->D agency spectrum is also a governance spectrum, from European-democratic to
authoritarian-automatic. Lean into it:

| Cell | Real seed | The speculative jump (what makes it a FUTURE) |
|---|---|---|
| A dialogic, shared | EU energy community + collective self-consumption | the AI actively brokers the shared pool and the community holds binding rights; mediation is automated, not a forum |
| B AI-follow, private | EU dynamic pricing + smart meter + your-data-your-choice | a personal grid agent plans your whole week, but you keep a veto; the market logic is internalised into one assistant |
| C delegation, shared | EU demand response, made collective + China automatic load control | a binding regional Climate Protocol: the building MUST stay under an allocation or the block is cut; you ratify within tight bounds, exceptions pre-filed |
| D AI-first, private | China automatic load control (Shenzhen-style) imported to the home | the grid sets and applies YOUR home's limit automatically, no opt-out, a reference code and a compliance state |

The jump in every case is the same move: take what Europe does by consent today and make it
compulsory, automatic, and embedded under a climate emergency. That single shift (consent -> mandate)
is what makes these read as futures, and it is the contradiction that drives the discussion: a
consent culture living under a Chinese-style automatic regime.

## Concrete elements to add (grounded, not corny)

Authority texture for C and D (no scores; the user ruled those out, use binary status and notices):
- Attribute the hard decisions to a "Regional Grid Authority" / "Climate Protocol", with ARKI as
  the local agent that applies it. Real systems have an operator above the app; naming it adds
  weight and removes the "an app decided my life" implausibility.
- A status line, not a score: "Within allocation" / "Allocation applied" / "Block at risk".
- "Automatic load control active 17:00-23:00" (a real term) as the header state on alert days.
- C: the advance-exception bureaucracy already in the spec (filed by a deadline, none on file).
- D: the applied notice with a reference code already in the spec; add "no opt-out during the
  alert" plainly.

Democratic texture for A and B (keep them clearly NOT authoritarian, so the contrast lands):
- A: frame the building as a registered energy community with a shared pool it governs; ARKI
  brokers. Consent and mutual aid, not compliance.
- B: "your data, your plan" framing; a dynamic tariff (peak 2.3x) you can spend into; you can
  always deny the AI. Market choice, not mandate.

## Keep it futuristic, not realistic (the dial)

- Push the jump, but only one step. Compulsory + automatic + embedded is the jump; do not also add
  flying drones or brain implants. One impossible-today thing per world keeps it believable.
- Date it far enough out (2035+) that compulsory allocation is plausible under repeated heat
  emergencies, but keep the furniture mundane (a building app, a notice, a vote).
- Let the friction stay unresolved (speculative design provokes, it does not solve): the studio
  still cooks, 4A still goes short, the block can still be cut. (Speculative design as social
  dreaming, per the 2026 field guidance.)

## What this buys the study

It sharpens the headline: the four futures become a probe of whether Europeans would accept a
Chinese-style automatic energy regime, and under what conditions (the AI-as-authority question your
pilot surfaced). C and D are literally "China today, your tomorrow"; A and B are the democratic
alternative. The mapping/preference exercise then reads as: where on the consent->mandate spectrum
do people draw the line, and why.
