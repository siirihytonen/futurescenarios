# Future paths: how each society reached its ARKI

The backing design-fiction scenarios for the four cooling prototypes (A, B, C, D). Each one
explains how a single 2033 society came to govern household cooling the way it does, and how
ARKI came to hold its particular role. These are the world-building layer. Each distils into a
short, "you"-voiced world card that the prototype actually shows; the long version lives here so
the study team can see the reasoning behind the card. The four are independent societies, not
one world's timeline. The same household lives the same heat-alert day in each, and only the
society around it changes.

## What is held constant (the control)

The day is identical in all four cells, so that the only thing a participant can react to is the
future path and ARKI's posture, not the situation.

- A heat-alert Friday. Flat 4C, 62 m2, west-facing. You, your partner, a dog.
- Your partner runs a home audio-mixing studio. A client session is booked 18:00 to 20:00 and
  needs 24 degrees to work.
- Saved preference: 22 degrees. Peak window: 17:00 to 23:00.
- The binding cap is the grid limit, not ARKI. On an alert day it holds the flat near 27 degrees
  through the peak window.
- The outcome is unfavourable in every cell. You never reach 22 degrees, and the studio never
  reaches 24 degrees. What changes across the four is who decides, who is present, and how the
  decision reaches you, never whether you get what you want.
- 4A, the neighbour in the shared cells, is a peer with an ordinary stake: a home office and a
  client call during peak. No medical need, no vulnerable party. The bind is among equals.

## The two axes

Agency (do you decide, or does ARKI) and community (is the resource shared and others visible, or
is it your private account). The four cells sit one in each quadrant.

|  | Shared (others visible) | Private (your account only) |
|---|---|---|
| **High agency** (you set, ARKI follows) | **A** The Neighbourhood Grid | **B** Your Own Meter |
| **Low agency** (ARKI sets, you respond) | **C** The Protocol | **D** Automatic Load Control |

The scenarios are written CIFS-style: a fixed near-future vantage (this Friday, 2033), grounded
in real signals with dates, a retrospective account of how the world arrived, then the day itself
in present tense. No probabilities are assigned and no path is preferred.

## A — The Neighbourhood Grid
**consent x commons. High agency, shared.**

> Since 2021, EU law has let citizens form energy communities and share the electricity they
> generate collectively. — Clean Energy for All Europeans package

**How it happened.** For most of the 2020s the citizen-energy-community rights written into EU
law in 2021 went almost unused, a clause for enthusiasts. Failure changed that. Through the
brownout summers of the late 2020s cooling demand outran a half-built grid, and most evenings the
utility offered an apology and little else. Neighbourhoods that could afford to stopped waiting.
With municipal grants they pooled the rooftop solar, the basement batteries and their single grid
connection, then registered as legal energy communities with a membership and a charter. A
community like that needs an operator for its shared pool. After the third failed summer the
assemblies hired an AI to run it. Bringing ARKI in was never a resident's private decision. The
building voted on it, and the vote sits in the bylaws. By 2033, running your block as a co-op with
an AI holding the shared meter is ordinary in these neighbourhoods.

**This Friday.** By four the flat is 29 degrees and the dog has moved to the bathroom tiles. The
building screen shows what it always shows on an alert day: one pool, fourteen homes, and the
amount left in the shared reserve. 4A has a client call at six and has asked the community for a
little more. Your partner has a session at the same hour. The studio needs 24 degrees and will not
get it. The pool cannot cover both, and because ARKI only brokers, the decision comes back to the
fourteen of you. You can hold your cooling where it is, pass some of your share to 4A, or take what
you set and leave 4A short. ARKI puts the numbers on the screen and waits for a person to move.
Everyone can see what everyone takes.

**The bind.** A commons dilemma among peers. The reserve is finite and shared with named
neighbours; cooling for your studio draws from the same pool 4A is counting on. Both claims are
ordinary and legitimate, and restraint is voluntary and visible.

## B — Your Own Meter
**consent x market. High agency, private.**

> From September 2025, users of connected products can access and port the data those products
> generate. — EU Data Act

**How it happened.** Europe's mainstream path never turned. It deepened. The smart meters rolled
out across the 2020s, and the dynamic tariffs that came with them, made every home a live account.
Two decisions then set the shape of things. The Draghi report of 2024 told Europe it was losing the
technology race, and the Apply AI strategy that followed in 2025 pushed adoption ahead of caution
across the economy. The Data Act, in force from 2025, gave you a right to the data your own devices
produced, which meant a company could train an agent on it for you. A market of personal energy
agents grew out of that. By the late 2020s owning one was as normal as a banking app. For the heat
the state kept a lighter instrument than its neighbours used: a cap on each home on alert days, set
by the grid, with everything inside the cap left to you and your agent. By 2033 this is how you run
the flat. Your data, your call.

**This Friday.** By four the flat is 29 degrees and climbing. You open ARKI and set the evening
yourself, the way you do on every alert day: the hours, the temperature you want, 24 degrees for
the studio while the session runs. Only then does ARKI answer. It holds five summers of this flat,
your calendar, and a smartwatch that noticed you sleep badly above 26 degrees, and it reads your
plan back against the grid limit and your weekly budget. The limit is the limit. On an alert day
the flat holds at 27 degrees through the peak whatever you set, so the studio will not reach 24
degrees between six and eight. ARKI offers its own version, 27 degrees easing to 26 for the session
and inside your budget, about as close to cool as tonight gets. You can take that, or keep the plan
you wrote and let it run nine kilowatt-hours over, a hotter Sunday or money at peak in exchange for
having asked. Either way the room stays warm. What is yours is the asking, and the bill.

**The bind.** A private, intertemporal trade. You hold full authorship and a veto, and the cap
still holds you at 27 degrees tonight. Your only real lever is how much of next week you spend on a
degree you will not get. No neighbour gains or loses by your choice.

## C — The Protocol
**mandate x commons. Low agency, shared.**

> In 2022 the EU required member states to cut gross electricity use, with binding reductions in
> peak hours. — Council Regulation 2022/1854

**How it happened.** This society had the same tools as the others and reached for the hardest one
after a catastrophe. In the heat-dome summer of the late 2020s a regional grid failed for nine
hours at the peak of an alert. Cooling stopped, the lifts stopped, hospital systems ran on
generators, and people died in the heat. The inquiry that followed was blunt: voluntary demand
response was too slow and too unreliable for a climate that now produced such days every year.
Governments already held a precedent. In 2022 the EU had compelled member states to cut peak
electricity during the gas crisis, and that emergency logic was now made permanent. Under the
Regional Grid Protocol each building receives one capped allocation on alert days and must stay
under it or the feeder is shed. AI does the dividing, because nothing else is fast enough to finish
before peak. Unions and community-energy groups won a single concession: the allocation is ratified
rather than simply imposed, so buildings vote within tight bounds and file any exception in advance.
By 2033 the Protocol is just how an alert day runs.

**This Friday.** The building has read *Within allocation* since noon, and it must stay there or
the block is cut at seven. The region's share arrives as one number, and ARKI divides it across the
fourteen homes before peak. Your part is narrow. You can accept tonight's plan, accept it with a
note, or call a review, though the review will not touch tonight and the window to file an exception
closed on Thursday at eight. Your flat holds at 27 degrees. Your partner's six o'clock session needs
a 24 it will not see. On the rail the other thirteen homes are voting, and most of them are short
too. You accept, because the alternative is the whole block dark by seven. The plan keeps the
building under the limit, and it is not yours to change tonight.

**The bind.** Collective good against your own voice. The allocation genuinely spares everyone a
block-wide cut, and your only move is to ratify it or call a review that changes nothing tonight.
The exception window has already closed.

## D — Automatic Load Control
**mandate x market. Low agency, private.**

> China's plan embeds at least five large AI models in the power grid by 2027; 2026 pilots cut peak
> demand by about 3.5 GW. — NDRC and reporting

**How it happened.** Faced with the same failing summers, this society looked outward rather than
inward. On China's grid the NDRC had ordered large AI models built into generation and dispatch by
2027, and cities like Shenzhen had already shown the method worked. Automatic load control trimmed
demand at homes hundreds of times a season without asking, and it held through peaks that would once
have caused blackouts. When the heatwaves at home turned blackouts from emergency into routine, the
government that had once ruled such control out found that ruling it out had become expensive. It
declared a heat emergency and then never lifted it. Inside that standing emergency the consent rules
were set aside through emergency powers and data carve-outs, and the household vote went with them. The utility now sets
each home's alert-day limit through ARKI, its local agent, and applies it automatically. There is no
building layer above you, and during the alert there is no opt-out. By 2033 the morning allocation
notice is wallpaper.

**This Friday.** The notice was on the screen when you woke. "Applied 06:20. 4C. Peak cooling 27
degrees, 17:00 to 23:00. Ref ARKI-4C-0619." You did not ask for it, and you cannot undo it. On alert
days the limit is set for you and applied before the kettle is on. Your home is a single account
here, with an allocation and nothing else on the screen. Your partner's session at six needs 24
degrees, which the notice does not record. You can log a request; the small print says the allocation
stands during the alert. The dog pants on the tiles. The grid holds, the lights stay on, and none of
it passed through you.

**The bind.** Reliability against autonomy. The automatic system demonstrably keeps the grid up and
is even-handed by formula, and you had no say over your own home and cannot opt out.

## Axis placement at a glance

| Cell | Agency | Community | ARKI's posture | The bind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | High: you set and act; ARKI only brokers | Shared: pool, 14 homes, 4A visible, mutual aid | Broker and facilitator | Commons dilemma among peers |
| B | High: you author first, keep the veto | Private: your budget, no one else affected | Assistant that follows | You own the cost, not the outcome |
| C | Low: ARKI allocates; you ratify within bounds | Shared: 14 homes vote, block-wide stake | Delegator | Collective good vs your voice |
| D | Low: applied automatically, no opt-out | Private: single account, nothing shared | Applies alone, notifies | Reliability vs autonomy |

The contrast pairs the study depends on: A and B differ only in community, C and D differ only in
community, A and C differ only in agency, B and D differ only in agency.

## Sources behind the signals

- EU Clean Energy for All Europeans package: citizen energy communities and collective
  self-consumption, in force since 2021.
- EU Data Act: data access and portability for connected products, applicable from September 2025.
- Council Regulation (EU) 2022/1854: emergency intervention including reductions in gross and
  peak-hour electricity use, 2022.
- Draghi report on European competitiveness (2024) and the EU Apply AI strategy (2025).
- China NDRC plan for large AI models embedded in the power grid by 2027; Shenzhen automatic
  load-control actions 2023 to 2025; roughly 3.5 GW of peak demand cut in 2026 pilots.
- See also `docs/2026-06-17-smartgrid-benchmark.md` for the real-now baseline and the speculative
  jump each cell takes past it.
